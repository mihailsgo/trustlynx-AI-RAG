# Do I need to log in to sign?

**No.** If you are the person signing a document, you never see a login screen.

## How it actually works

The pad is signed in once — by the organisation that owns it, not by you. That
session belongs to the device and stays in place across visitors. When you walk up
to the tablet, it is already authenticated and ready.

That is deliberate. Asking every visitor to create an account just to sign one form
would defeat the point of the product.

## Why you might see an email address on screen

Some setups display the account the device is signed in as, usually in a corner of
the screen. If you see an email address and an organisation name there, that is the
**device's** account — the organisation's — not yours. It is there so staff can
confirm the pad is connected to the right place.

## Is my identity checked?

PadSign does not verify who you are. It does not check an identity document, and it
does not look you up in any official register. What it captures is the signature you
draw plus, in some setups, a name supplied by the system that sent the document.

If your setup shows a **Signer: <name>** line above the signature box, that name
came from the sending system — a customer record, for example. It is there so *you*
can check the document is meant for you before signing. If that name is wrong, do
not sign; tell the person at the desk.

## For staff

If a member of staff sees a login screen or an "Application server is not
available" message on the pad, that is a different situation — the device session
or the connection needs attention rather than anything the visitor did.
