# Changing your mind, refusing, and fixing mistakes

The one fact that drives everything here: **nothing is submitted until you tap Sign.** Before
that moment you are only reading and typing on a screen. After that moment there is a signed,
sealed document. The two situations are completely different.

## Before you tap Sign

**You can refuse.** Signing is your decision. If you do not want to sign, hand the device
back and say so. Nothing has been recorded against you — an unsigned document simply clears
itself and no half-completed anything is filed.

**You can disagree or ask questions.** The document comes from the organisation in front of
you, so they are the ones to raise it with. If something in it is wrong or unclear, say so
before signing — they can correct and resend the document in minutes.

**You can take more time.** Read as long as you need. If the document clears itself while you
are still reading (it does so after roughly ten minutes of waiting), that is not a problem —
ask for it to be sent again.

**You can fix your own typing.** Tap back into any field and correct it. Check your entries —
especially contact details — before signing; it is the cheapest moment to fix them.

## After you tap Sign

A signed document **cannot be edited**. That is a feature, not a limitation: the seal exists
precisely so that nothing in the file can change afterwards without it being detectable.
There is no undo button inside PadSign, and staff cannot "just correct" a signed PDF.

So if you notice a mistake after signing — a wrong name, a wrong figure, a box you did not
mean to tick — **tell the person at the desk straight away**. The remedy is straightforward:
the organisation reissues the document, corrected, and you sign the new version. The flawed
first version does not disappear from their records by itself, so also ask them to note that
it was superseded.

Whether you can legally *withdraw* consent or cancel an agreement you signed is a different
question entirely — that is between you and the organisation under whatever terms and law
apply, and PadSign has no part in it.

## Signing for someone else

Sometimes the person named on the document is not the person holding the pad — a parent for
a child, someone with power of attorney, a colleague "just signing quickly".

PadSign does not check who is holding the device, so the system will not stop this — which is
exactly why you should be careful with it. If your setup shows a **Signer:** name above the
signature box and it is not you, do not sign without telling staff first. Whether a
representative's signature is acceptable is the organisation's call (and sometimes a legal
one), so the honest route is always to say who you are and let them decide how to record it.
