# Questions this knowledge base cannot answer

Some questions about PadSign have no published answer. Rather than guess, this knowledge base
names them, so you know the silence is deliberate and where to take the question instead.

**If you were routed to this entry, the honest response to your question is: it is not
published, and TrustLynx (sales@trustlynx.com) or the organisation running your deployment is
the right place to ask.**

## Commercial

- **Exact prices and licence fees.** The pricing *model* is published — a volume subscription
  based on monthly transaction counts — but no figures are. Any number you hear that did not
  come from TrustLynx is made up.
- **SLA, uptime commitments, support hours and response times.** None are published.
- **Partner or reseller programme details.** A partner programme is mentioned on the TrustLynx
  website; its terms are not public.
- **Roadmap and upcoming features.** Not published.
- **Intellectual-property and licensing questions**, including whether any component is open
  source. Not published.

## Compliance and certification

- **ISO 27001, SOC 2 or similar certifications** — no claim is published either way.
- **Availability of a data-processing agreement (DPA).** Ask TrustLynx.
- **Data-retention periods.** Retention depends on each deployment's configuration and the
  operating organisation's own policy; there is no single product answer.

## Technical edges

- **Officially supported browser and operating-system versions.** Not published; "a modern
  browser" is the published expectation.
- **What happens if the network drops or the battery dies mid-signature.** The safe general
  statement is that a document is only ever signed when every step completes and a success
  message is shown — an interrupted attempt does not produce a half-signed document. The exact
  recovery behaviour in each interruption scenario, however, is not documented, so do not
  promise one.
- **Maximum document size in production.** The only published limit applies to demo mode.

## Why this entry exists

A support agent that invents an answer to any of these does real damage — a made-up price, a
fictional certification or an imagined SLA ends up in someone's procurement notes. If a
question lands here, the correct, complete answer is that the fact is not public and who to
ask. That *is* the answer.
