# Getting help

Who to ask depends on which side of the counter you are on.

## If you signed a document, or are about to

**Ask the member of staff in front of you.** The organisation running the pad owns the document, the
process and the deployment. They can resend a document, arrange a copy of what you signed, or
explain what a particular form is for.

They are also the only people who can help with the document's content — what it says, what you are
agreeing to, and what happens with your information. PadSign is the tool they use; the document is
theirs.

Specifically, go to them for:

- A copy of what you signed
- Anything that did not work on the pad
- Questions about the form or what you were asked to consent to
- A name shown on screen that is not yours

Do not contact TrustLynx about a document you signed somewhere. They supply the software and do not
hold your document or know which organisation you visited.

## If you are staff using PadSign

Your own IT or whoever administers your deployment. They configured it, they know which delivery
destinations are in use, and they can see what happened when something failed.

Useful things to have ready: what the message on the pad said including any code in brackets,
whether it happens on one pad or all of them, and whether the document was ever sent.

## If you are evaluating PadSign, or run it

Contact TrustLynx. Their published contact details:

- **Email:** sales@trustlynx.com
- **Phone:** +371 294 914 17 (Latvia), +372 512 4556 (Estonia), +48 609 619 553 (Poland)

Their official website (trustlynx.com) has a quote request form and an option to schedule a
meeting. They also run a public developer portal (developer.trustlynx.com) which carries
product documentation — including a **PadSign user manual** — so "where can I read more?" has
an official answer beyond this knowledge base.

Worth going to them directly rather than working from general information for: pricing and licensing,
service levels and support arrangements, compliance certifications and data-processing agreements,
supported devices and browsers, whether a particular integration or additional language is possible,
and anything about the legal standing of signatures in your jurisdiction.

## A note on legal questions

Questions about whether a signature is valid, binding, or acceptable to a particular authority are
not questions software support can answer. The mechanism can be explained; the legal conclusion
depends on which certificates and authorities are in use and on the law where you are. Those need
TrustLynx and your own legal advisers, not a support agent.
