# Accessibility, and what to do if signing is physically difficult

An honest answer, because this is an area where the published information has real limits.

## What exists today

- **Dark mode.** The interface follows the device's own light-or-dark setting automatically,
  which some people find easier on the eyes.
- **Larger buttons.** The size of the button text on the signature controls is an option the
  organisation can turn up, giving bigger touch targets.
- **A stylus works** as well as a finger. For anyone who finds finger-drawing awkward, a
  stylus usually helps considerably.
- **Unlimited retries.** The signature can be cleared and redrawn as many times as needed, and
  the box accepts several separate strokes — nothing requires one smooth motion.
- **No time pressure while a document is open.** Nothing is submitted until Sign is tapped.
  One caveat: an unsigned document does clear itself after roughly ten minutes, so if more
  time is needed, say so — staff can simply send it again.

## What is not published

There is **no published accessibility conformance level** (such as WCAG) for the signing
screen, and screen-reader behaviour is not documented. If your organisation has a formal
accessibility requirement, put it to TrustLynx directly rather than assuming either way.

## If signing on the device is difficult for you

Tell the member of staff in front of you. This matters more than any setting:

- **A signature that looks shaky or imperfect is still your signature.** The mark does not
  need to be beautiful or to match your paper signature stroke for stroke.
- **You can take another attempt** — Clear and redraw, as often as you need.
- **Ask for the device to be handed to you** in whatever position works — flat on the counter,
  held, or angled. The signature box works the same way regardless of how the tablet is held,
  and nothing about the layout assumes a right-handed signer, but a comfortable position makes
  the drawing far easier.
- **If reading the screen is the problem**, ask staff to read the document to you or to
  provide a paper copy to read before signing on the device. The document is theirs; they can
  present it however serves you.
- **If you cannot reasonably use the device at all**, say so. The organisation chose PadSign
  as a convenience, not a barrier — how they accommodate you (including on paper) is their
  process, and you are entitled to ask.

## For organisations evaluating PadSign

If accessibility conformance is a procurement requirement, ask TrustLynx for a current
statement — this knowledge base deliberately does not claim a level that has not been
published. Practical accommodations available out of the box are the ones listed above.
