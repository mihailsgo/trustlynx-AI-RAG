# How is PadSign different from services like emailed e-signature links?

They solve different problems. The distinction is *where the signer is*.

## Remote e-signature services

You upload a document, the platform emails a link, and the other party signs it
whenever and wherever they like, in the provider's web application. The document
passes through and is stored on the provider's platform.

That works well when the signer is somewhere else and time is flexible.

## PadSign

The signer is standing in front of you — at your counter, at their door, in your
waiting room. There is no email, no link, and no waiting. The document appears on
your device, they sign it, and it is done before they leave.

## Three practical differences

**The signer is present.** You can see who signed. There is no question of whether
the right person opened the email. If your setup shows the signer-name label, they
can also confirm the document is meant for them before signing.

**It runs on your infrastructure.** TrustLynx deploys its products on your own
controlled infrastructure — cloud or on-premises — behind your firewalls,
specifically so you are not sharing data with a third party. The documents stay in
your environment.

**The document comes from your systems.** PadSign generates the document from your
own templates via your CRM, service management or document management system,
rather than you manually uploading files one at a time.

## Choosing between them

If your signers are remote and asynchronous, a remote signing service fits. If
they are physically in front of you, PadSign removes the paper without adding an
email round-trip. Plenty of organisations have both situations and can use both
approaches; they are not mutually exclusive.

TrustLynx also offers other products in this space, including an API platform,
system connectors, SignBox, and a standalone e-sealing service, so the in-person
case is one part of a broader range.
